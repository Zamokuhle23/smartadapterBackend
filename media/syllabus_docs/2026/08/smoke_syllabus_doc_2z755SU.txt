TOPIC 1: ALGEBRA

Simplifying algebraic fractions. An algebraic fraction is a quotient of two
algebraic expressions, for example 6x over 9. To simplify an algebraic fraction,
factorise the numerator and the denominator completely, then cancel common
factors. For example, 6x/9 simplifies to 2x/3 because 3 is a common factor of
both 6x and 9. When the numerator and denominator are polynomials, factorise
each first: (x^2 - 9)/(x + 3) becomes ((x-3)(x+3))/(x+3) = x - 3.

Operations with algebraic fractions follow the same rules as numeric fractions:
to add, rewrite with a common denominator; to multiply, cancel before expanding;
to divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

Factorising quadratic trinomials. A quadratic trinomial has the form ax^2 + bx + c.
To factorise x^2 + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.
For example, x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 6)? No: check that 2 and 3 multiply to 6
and add to 5, so x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3). When a =/= 1, use splitting the
middle term or the AC method.

TOPIC 2: EQUATIONS AND INEQUALITIES

Linear equations: collect like terms, isolate the variable using inverse
operations, and always verify by substituting your solution back into the
original equation. Quadratic equations may be solved by factorising, completing
the square, or the quadratic formula x = (-b +/- sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a).
The discriminant b^2 - 4ac tells you the nature of the roots.
